# OutageHub founding sponsorship motion

## Commercial framing

OutageHub is seeking one paid CAD $10,000 founding sponsorship. It is not
seeking a charitable donation, does not promise a tax receipt, and must never
describe the payment as tax deductible. The sponsor receives defined benefits:
recognition, bounded API use, and relevant coverage briefings. Data treatment
and published conclusions remain independent.

This distinction matters in Canada. The CRA describes sponsorship as a payment
where the business receives recognition or promotion in return, and notes that
such benefits affect whether a payment is a charitable gift:

- https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/operating-a-registered-charity/receiving-gifts/sponsorship.html
- https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/operating-a-registered-charity/receiving-gifts/what-a-gift.html

This document is an outreach policy, not tax or legal advice. Any agreement and
tax treatment should be reviewed by the company's accountant or counsel.

## What the evidence supports

Fundraising research does support naming a specific amount. A natural field
experiment found that a suggested amount materially increased the number of
people who gave. That result comes from charitable individual giving, however;
it does not prove that a cold CAD $10,000 corporate request will convert:

- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2014.02.002

Corporate sponsorship is a shared-value purchase rather than pure generosity.
Best Buy's published corporate-funding process asks first for eligibility and
fit, then audience, timing, activation or visibility, measurable outcomes, and
the shared value created for the company and community:

- https://apply.bestbuyfoundation.org/submit

Therefore the email should:

1. Establish the human founder story and the already-operating public resource.
2. Use one verified company fact.
3. Explain a concrete way the data or public-interest work connects to the
   company before asking for money.
4. Name CAD $10,000 plainly.
5. Offer a bounded, company-relevant benefit without selling influence.
6. Ask one low-friction question to the likely approver or internal router.

The first email earns a conversation. A one-page scope should carry the exact
deliverables, benefit limits, independence terms, invoice path, timing, and
measurement plan.

## Budget-authority routing

The campaign distinguishes:

- **Executive approver:** founder, owner, CEO, president, general manager, CFO,
  COO, or managing partner at a smaller company.
- **Programme owner:** sponsorship, partnerships, community investment,
  community relations, or corporate affairs leader.
- **Senior budget router:** VP/director/head in marketing, communications,
  strategy, or innovation.
- **Budget router:** manager, coordinator, adviser, or business-development
  person near the relevant programme.

A title never proves that CAD $10,000 is currently available. Copy may ask the
person to discuss or route the sponsorship; it may not call them the budget
owner without direct evidence.

## Sponsor versus API customer

A company may sponsor OutageHub and later buy the API, but these are separate
commercial decisions. Spruce Leaf enforces the following boundary:

- no concurrent sponsorship and API-sales outreach to one organization;
- if the clearest reason to approach the company is its own operational need,
  use the customer motion first;
- sponsorship does not prepay, discount, or obligate a future API contract;
- any later API sale requires separate scope, pricing, procurement, and terms.

This avoids positioning a high-probability buyer as a benefactor while still
allowing a genuine sponsor relationship to develop into an ordinary customer
relationship later.
