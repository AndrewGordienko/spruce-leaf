# OutageHub sales manual

Status: working doctrine for founder-led, manually supervised selling
Motion: verified location and workflow → useful outage artifact → small reply → evaluation
North star: every message should help the recipient understand or improve an operational decision, even if they never buy OutageHub.

This manual is a copy-policy input. If code, a prompt, a playbook, a gold example, or a reviewer conflicts with it, the safer rule in this manual wins and the conflict must be fixed.

## Doctrine

1. **Sell operational certainty, not outage data.** OutageHub matters only when utility context improves a real decision: diagnose, dispatch, escalate, notify, investigate, place a generator, attribute downtime, or wait for restoration.
2. **The recipient owes us nothing.** Earn every next step. Never assume interest, manufacture familiarity or urgency, guilt a recipient, or disguise a meeting request as a question.
3. **No evidence means no outreach.** An industry label or homepage summary is not enough. Require a relevant public location, a plausible outage-time workflow, a current trigger or responsibility, supported role fit, and a useful contribution.
4. **Give before asking.** A sendable first touch provides or offers a result OutageHub can really produce: a location replay, coverage analysis, integration sketch, sample API response, dispatch-cost input model, or incident-triage checklist.
5. **Use hypotheses, not internal claims.** State public facts as facts. State an internal workflow only as a bounded question. Do not convert exposure into waste, downtime, truck rolls, ownership, or urgency.
6. **Disqualification is success.** Stop when outage attribution is infrequent, already solved, irrelevant to the person, unsupported by a consent basis, or not worth operational change.
7. **The first email does not ask for a demo.** It earns a correction, workflow confirmation, routing answer, or acceptance of a useful artifact.

## Acquisition channels and lifecycle

Spruce Leaf currently realizes first contact through email and LinkedIn under the governed [channel doctrine](acquisition-channels.md). Cold research, conferences, webinars, referrals, partners, and existing customers have separate source records and message contracts. A named relationship owner remains visible through qualification, presales, proof, deployment, customer success, and expansion.

The seven-stage customer journey is research/channel selection → first contact → useful proof or event interaction → qualification → presales application design → pilot/commercial decision → deployment/retention/expansion. These are account states, not seven emails.

OutageHub should describe the decision improved, not merely the API.
- [PostHog's outbound handbook](https://posthog.com/handbook/growth/sales/outbound-sales) treats outbound as tightly focused marketing: research first, qualify or disqualify, earn each step, and give useful material such as documentation, a code sample, a Loom, or a trial.
- [PostHog's use-case selling guide](https://posthog.com/handbook/growth/use-case-selling/use-case-selling) organizes plays around jobs, personas, signals, present pain, desired state, impact, proof, and evaluation.
- [GitLab's Command of the Message](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/sales/command-of-the-message/) and [discovery guidance](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/sales/playbook/discovery/) lead with the buyer's value driver and why now, then examine the problem, current process, people affected, and consequences.
- [Tomorrow.io's Hitachi story](https://www.tomorrow.io/customer-stories/how-tomorrow-io-powers-hitachis-smarter-vegetation-management-for-utilities-and-rail/) shows data creating value through decisions such as routing crews, changing work orders, and prioritizing risk.
- [Gong's executive cold-email analysis](https://www.gong.io/blog/do-execs-really-reply-to-cold-email-here-s-what-the-data-says) supports short, customer-language messages and a useful offer instead of a reflexive meeting request.

These sources guide doctrine; competitor claims and case studies are not proof of OutageHub results.

## Required Sendable Brief

Writing is forbidden until one prospect record contains all of the following:

- selected use-case play;
- operational workflow and decision;
- atomic evidence claim IDs and source URLs;
- relevant trigger and why now;
- current likely workaround, explicitly marked as confirmed or inferred;
- plausible operational consequence;
- recipient relationship to the decision, supported by person-specific evidence or marked as adjacent/router;
- useful give-back that is already complete or can be produced from named inputs;
- message objective and expected reply;
- consent basis, captured evidence, capture date, and role relevance;
- uncertainties that the message must not state as facts;
- compliance review status.

If any field is generic, absent, stale, contradictory, or unsupported, hold the account.

## First-touch lanes

### Proof ready

A location-specific historical result already exists. T1 leads with that result and offers the exact record or replay. Do not save the strongest evidence for a follow-up.

### Discovery ready

A public location and relevant decision exist, but no historical result is complete. T1 offers to produce one specified result and asks one operating or routing question answerable in seconds.

### Hold

No unique evidence, no useful give-back, weak role fit, or incomplete consent evidence means no email.

After a real reply, create an application brief for the outage-attribution use case: current workflow, decision, why the current approach fails, affected roles, OutageHub's bounded role, integration options, systems/data, historical replay, success measures, first paid scope, customer inputs, owners, constraints, and expansion path. The email earns permission for this presales work; it does not replace it.

## Message structures

Choose the structure that best serves the brief; do not force a universal template.

- **Proof first:** completed location result → operating implication → one correction or routing question.
- **Useful artifact:** verified location/workflow → exactly what will be returned → one easy operating question.
- **Operating question first:** specific decision question → public location reason → concrete result offered.
- **Correction request:** bounded public inference → one-line correction path → optional artifact.
- **Routing request:** public responsibility → precise decision → ask for the owner, without pitching a meeting.

T1 should normally be 50–100 words, use plain operational language, contain one question, and never make a discovery call the only response path.

## Seven-stage account motion

Seven stages describe the account process, not seven consecutive emails to one person. Most prospects receive T1 and no more than two useful follow-ups. An unusually strong, engaged account can use:

1. Day 0 — initial email testing one operating hypothesis.
2. Day 3 — LinkedIn touch that adds familiarity without repeating the pitch.
3. Day 5 — evidence email with a new replay, coverage result, or finding.
4. Day 9 — practical follow-up with a new integration sketch, checklist, or API example.
5. Day 13 — multithreading decision; another role may be contacted only for a distinct, evidence-backed objective.
6. Day 18 — decision email asking whether the workflow is real, solved, or irrelevant.
7. Day 25 — close the account thread and record the outcome.

No individual receives more than five emails, and evidence will normally authorize fewer. Every stage has its own condition, role, evidence/artifact IDs, manual approval, and stopping rule. Multithreading never means sending identical copy to several employees.

## Hard rejection

Reject product-first introductions, generic industry personalization, unsupported workflow or ownership claims, fabricated proof, meeting requests before interest, sender-benefit closers, defensive caveats that dominate the email, or a message without a concrete useful output. Reject repeated openings, sentence structures, questions, and calls to action across the current batch.

Phrases such as “an answer would help,” “your perspective would be useful,” “would love to learn,” and “trying to understand” are deterministic failures.

## Compliance and CASL

This manual is operational policy, not legal advice. Canadian counsel must approve the program before prospect delivery.

The [CRTC compliance guide](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm) says commercial electronic messages generally require consent, identification information, and an unsubscribe mechanism. Conspicuous publication may support implied consent only under defined conditions, including no statement against unsolicited messages and relevance to the person's business role. The sender must be able to prove the basis relied upon. A purchased or enriched list is not itself consent evidence.

For each recipient retain the source URL, capture timestamp, relevant published address/context, no-solicitation check, role-relevance explanation, basis category, and reviewer. Live transport also requires sender identification, a mailing address, unsubscribe handling, exact-copy approval, and the controlled-inbox allowlist while the supervised pilot lock remains active.

Do not generate, approve, schedule, or send prospect copy with a missing or rejected compliance record.

## Validation before external sending

Generate at least 20 real OutageHub messages, review them blind, and require every one to pass factual and compliance checks. At least 20 must be manually approved unchanged, and controlled SMTP delivery must pass. The first prospect pilot is manual, one contact per company, and evaluated on human reply, positive reply, correction, referral, artifact request, meeting, and qualified opportunity—not opens.
