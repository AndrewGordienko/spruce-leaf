# OutageHub follow-up doctrine

A follow-up is justified by new recipient value, not elapsed time. Renaming “checking in” to “circling back” does not make it useful.

## Individual correspondence

Cold outreach starts with one message. After engagement, an individual may receive at most five email touches, and evidence should normally authorize fewer:

1. **Relevant hypothesis:** one verified observation, a bounded operating question, and a concrete contribution.
2. **Proof:** a new historical match, annotated result, sample ticket, API response, or other completed evidence. Never restate T1.
3. **Practical resource:** a newly adapted integration outline, coverage report, triage checklist, or sample payload.
4. **Decision:** use new buyer/evidence context to ask whether the problem is real, solved, or irrelevant.
5. **Close:** acknowledge timing may be wrong and offer an easy correction, redirect, defer, or stop path.

The account may also contain a non-repetitive LinkedIn touch and a separate multithreading decision, creating the seven-stage account motion in the sales manual. Those are not excuses to increase one person's email count.

Every follow-up stores a `value_add_type` and the new evidence or artifact ID. The value type must differ from previous touches. If no new value exists, do not send.

Stop immediately on a negative reply, unsubscribe, contradictory evidence, bad role fit, invalid or weakened consent basis, delivery failure, or evidence that the workflow is already adequately solved. Silence never authorizes an automatic cadence expansion.

The supervised pilot remains T1-only unless engagement and current policy explicitly authorize more.
