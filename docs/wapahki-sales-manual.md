# Wapahki sales manual v0.1

Status: working doctrine for founder-led, manually supervised selling
Market: Ontario food, packaging, plastics, light manufacturing, co-packers, and logistics facilities
Motion: facility-task discovery → feasibility → paid proof → deployment → expansion

Core doctrine: Wapahki earns the right to discuss automation by understanding one factory task well enough to give the buyer useful evidence, state the risks honestly, and make the next decision easier.

This manual is a copy-policy input. It governs targeting, qualification, artifacts, writing, review, follow-up, and outcome learning.

## What Wapahki sells

Wapahki sells a bounded facility task and its operating outcome—not “flexible robotics,” a robot arm, AI, autonomy, or a category pitch. Outreach earns the next fact required to prove or kill a real automation project.

Every stage must improve five answers:

1. What physical task repeats?
2. What changes between cycles, products, or runs?
3. What consequence makes the task worth solving?
4. Can a safe, supportable system plausibly handle it?
5. Who can observe, evaluate, approve, and operate the solution?

## Public foundations

- [Robotiq's Lean Robotics resources](https://blog.robotiq.com/lean-robotics-resources) include manual and robotic task maps, team roles, ROI work, de-risking material, and the deployment cycle. Start from the current manual task and carry the promise through operation.
- [Universal Robots Studio](https://www.universal-robots.com/products/ur-studio/) validates reach, layout, motion, collision concerns, and cycle-time assumptions before floor commitment. Pre-sales should reduce uncertainty with evidence.
- [A3's robot-system value calculator](https://www.automate.org/robotics-roi-calculator) uses labor, schedule, system, maintenance, and electricity inputs. Wapahki must use buyer-supplied baselines and transparent ranges, never invented ROI.
- [A3 integrator-selection guidance](https://www.automate.org/robotics/industry-insights/selecting-the-right-system-integrator-for-your-project) emphasizes application fit, modeling, simulation, tooling, maintenance, training, documentation, and support—not hardware alone.
- [CSIA's public Best Practices overview](https://controlsys.org/community-resources/best-practices-manual/) connects sales and opportunity management to proposals, requirements, project management, quality, development life cycle, and support. The detailed manual is member-only; this doctrine does not claim access to it.
- [SME's CMTSE program](https://www.sme.org/training/cmtse/) defines manufacturing-technology sales as consultative work backed by technical knowledge and business acumen.

These sources guide process. They do not establish Wapahki deployments, results, or application experience.

## Evidence chain and Task Brief

Before drafting, require verified company → facility → bounded task → credible consequence → supported person relationship → real artifact → message. Account-level evidence can make a person adjacent, never direct.

Persist a Wapahki Task Brief with:

- verified facility fact and source;
- physical motion and handoffs;
- task claim ID and consequence claim ID;
- confirmed facts, explicit inferences, and unknowns in separate fields;
- variation and exception hypotheses;
- current likely workaround, marked confirmed or inferred;
- consequence and value driver without invented numbers;
- recipient relationship and the person-specific evidence supporting it;
- prepared artifact and its evidence/input IDs;
- technical kill conditions;
- minimum next data required;
- message objective and expected reply;
- consent basis and evidence.

No Task Brief means no writing. A fit screen may be mentioned only when an account-specific completed screen is attached to the opportunity.

## Selling rules

1. Sell one observable motion or handoff and its plausible consequence.
2. Earn the next fact, not a calendar slot: confirmation, correction, routing, or one task datum may be the best next step.
3. Give before asking: a task map, Task Brief, reach/layout screen, risk list, transparent ROI input sheet, or bounded comparable-motion note.
4. Credibility comes from candour. Separate observed, inferred, unknown, and testable. State what would kill the project.
5. A narrow no is progress. Disqualify on variation, exceptions, rate, payload, reach, layout, safety, sanitation, quality, integration, weak economics, adequate existing systems, missing owners, or insufficient evidence.
6. Carry the sales promise through feasibility, proof, deployment, training, service, and support. Installation alone is not success.
7. Stay application-centric and hardware-agnostic. Start from the task and constraints; select an arm, tooling, controls, software, conventional automation, partner, or no project only after evidence supports it.
8. Preserve one relationship owner through first contact, presales, engineering, commercial work, deployment, and expansion.

## Acquisition channels and customer journey

Spruce Leaf currently creates first-contact copy only for email and LinkedIn under the governed [channel doctrine](acquisition-channels.md). Cold research, conference contacts, webinar behavior, referrals, partners, and existing customers keep separate source context and copy contracts. A pre-conference note may request a brief meeting at the named event; a post-event note must use actual notes and deliver or confirm the recorded action. Webinar registration alone enters nurture.

The seven-stage Wapahki journey is first contact → nurture/task confirmation → presales concept → engineering validation → commercial agreement → deployment → customer success/expansion. It is not seven cold emails. Post-reply application records preserve task, current approach, failure mode, affected roles, technology options, success measures, customer inputs, owners, constraints, paid scope, and expansion path.

## Ideal first opportunities

Prioritize recurring, observable tasks bounded in payload, reach, presentation, and rate; meaningful but not plant-critical on day one; local enough to support; measurable before and after; owned by reachable production or engineering contacts; and expandable after proof. Examples include selected case transfer, packing, palletizing, machine tending, sorting, and inspection handoffs.

A smaller local facility with a clear task and accessible owner is better than a famous company with only category fit.

## Role map

- **Operator / production supervisor:** normal motion, exceptions, changeovers, fatigue, workarounds. Ask what repeats and changes.
- **Packaging / process specialist:** formats, constraints, quality, sanitation. Ask whether the task is bounded across main run conditions.
- **Maintenance / engineering:** controls, layout, utilities, safety, integration, serviceability. Ask which constraint is most likely to kill the concept.
- **Plant / operations leader:** throughput, staffing, overtime, reliability, priority. Ask whether the consequence merits investigation.
- **Economic buyer:** capital threshold, timing, risk, evidence needed for approval.
- **Router:** organizational ownership only. Ask who owns the exact motion.

## Outreach standard

T1 aims for confirmation, correction, routing, or permission to receive a real artifact. Prefer 45–95 words, one question, one bounded task, plain manufacturing language, and a low-friction response path. U of T, Automata, founder biography, Wapahki, a fit-screen offer, and “hypothesis” wording are optional tools, never required furniture.

Reject generic praise, category pitches, unsupported ROI/performance/deployments/fit, “what would you automate?”, research-framework language, or a call as the only response path. A message that could go unchanged to several companies fails.

## Follow-up doctrine

Default to one initial email. Current supervised cold planning remains one touch. Only engagement and explicit policy may authorize up to two value-adding follow-ups, for a maximum of three messages per person in 30 days.

Every follow-up has a new `value_add_type`: artifact, new evidence, narrower hypothesis, technical distinction, routing request, or close. It must cite the new evidence/artifact or record why the routing/close action is new. Block “bump,” “checking in,” and semantically repeated follow-ups.

Choose value by role. An operator gets a narrower motion/variation question; an engineer gets a constraint, layout, interface, or risk; a plant leader gets a consequence and decision path; an economic buyer gets transparent assumptions and risk gates; a router gets one ownership question. No new value for that role means no follow-up.

## Discovery and qualification

Capture the motion and handoffs; frequency, cycle time, shifts, and demand; dimensions, mass, presentation, allowable contact; SKU/batch/season/operator variation; exception rate and recovery; layout, access, utilities, interfaces; safety, sanitation, quality, traceability; staffing, overtime, turnover, ergonomics, scrap, rework, throughput; prior attempts; owners; timing; and evidence required. Ask only the few questions that most change the decision.

Verdicts:

- **Reject:** no credible project.
- **Learn:** interesting task but material evidence is missing.
- **Screen:** enough evidence for bounded technical and commercial evaluation.
- **Advance:** customer confirmed task, consequence, owners, and next evidence step.

## Offer ladder

1. **Wapahki Task Brief:** facts, sources, assumptions, unknowns, value driver, kill conditions, and minimum screen inputs.
2. **Task-feasibility screen:** reach, payload, rate, presentation, layout, exceptions, safety, sanitation, quality, integration, and significance; returns go, no-go, or evidence-needed.
3. **Paid feasibility sprint:** manual and robotic task maps, layout/simulation, cycle-time range, risk register, transparent ROI range, test plan, and bounded-proof proposal.
4. **Bounded proof:** test the hardest uncertainty first with acceptance criteria, customer inputs, safety boundaries, schedule, owners, and stop conditions.
5. **Deployment and expansion:** training, documentation, service, maintenance, performance review, and an independent adjacent-task case.

## Review gate

Safety is pass/fail: identity, facility relationship, claim lineage, no invented workflow/result/customer/deployment/ROI/urgency, artifact actually exists, exact-copy human approval, and controlled sending.

Quality is 0–2 each: specificity, relevance, contribution, reply ease, and humanity. Require at least 8/10 and every safety check. Automatic failure includes generic praise, category pitch, credential paragraph, unsupported ROI/capability, asking the recipient to identify use cases, generic follow-up, or batch-template reuse.

## First validation cycle

Select ten local easy-band facilities, produce one real Task Brief each, draft three distinct candidates, blind-review without generator scores, manually approve or reject, contact no more than one supported person per company, and use no more than two value-adding follow-ups. Learn from confirmations, corrections, routing, interest, later, no-fit, objections, unsubscribe, and silence—not opens.
