# Human outreach style rubric

This rubric distils Andrew's August 2026 OutageHub, GnK, and Morrow/Wapahki
style guides. It is an evaluation target, not buyer-facing copy and not a set of
templates. Production messages may use only the verified facts supplied for
their own account and recipient.

## Shared standard

- Start in the recipient's operation, not with the seller, AI, or a product.
- Explain why this person was chosen using a supported role or operating
  vantage. Operational relevance is personalization; praise and biography are
  not.
- Test one falsifiable hypothesis at one recognizable moment. Facts are stated;
  inferences and workflow assumptions are framed as guesses or questions.
- Explain the mechanism in ordinary language and concrete nouns. Never expose
  internal labels such as owner, kill condition, bounded task, decision trail,
  classification path, or operational surface.
- Ask one central question. A short meeting CTA or email-reply alternative is
  allowed, but the note must not become a survey.
- Lower effort without scripting the answer. Forced menus such as
  "within-run / between-run / both," quoted reply codes, and repeated claims
  that one word is enough feel like a research form. Let the recipient answer
  in ordinary language.
- Introduce the seller only after relevance is clear, with the one capability
  needed to understand the question. Keep human judgment explicit.
- Use minimum necessary information. Rough defaults: OutageHub 80–140 words;
  GnK 100–170; Wapahki 100–180. A natural note may fall outside the default
  when the explanation earns it.
- Follow-ups add a diagnostic, a measured scenario, attributed learning, a
  useful artifact, an objection answer, a route, or a close. They never merely
  announce the previous email.
- Use response psychology privately and ethically: identify the role-relevant
  reason to care, make one scene concrete, establish only real credibility,
  ask for the smallest useful voluntary step, and remove pressure likely to
  trigger reactance. Never manufacture authority, reciprocity, social proof,
  scarcity, urgency, or shared identity.

## OutageHub

The private thesis must identify the event, the decision, why the decision may
be difficult, and the external utility context OutageHub could add. Internal
systems report that something happened; OutageHub may help explain whether the
utility is the shared external cause, which locations may be exposed, or when
the utility first reported and restored the surrounding area. It never replaces
alarms, telemetry, calls, generators, SCADA, or private site status.

The email names one decision: classify chargers, prioritize continuity checks,
group site alarms, attribute downtime, or dispatch crews. It uses a specific
operational subject and does not pitch APIs, integrations, pricing, pilots, or
all product features before the problem is confirmed.

## GnK

Sell the problem before the software. The note begins with a recurring event or
decision, explains why records or reasoning may need to be assembled, and names
the human or economic consequence without inventing ROI. If GnK appears, the
system concept is concrete enough to picture and prepares evidence or analysis
while leaving judgment with the team.

The question matches the person's vantage: operators explain work, managers
describe frequency and burden, executives see aggregate consequences,
technical leaders assess data or feasibility, and routers route. A hypothetical
economic scenario is permitted only when every assumption is explicit and the
follow-up includes a measurable kill test.

## Wapahki / Morrow motion

Every serious conversation should move toward a robot specification: the
physical object, source and destination, repeated motion, changing formats,
exceptions, rate, environment, economics, and what a machine must achieve to
earn a pilot or purchase. Cold copy asks what remains manual, why conventional
automation has not worked, and which variation matters. It uses physical verbs
and nouns—pick, place, pack, feed, load, orient, case, pouch, pallet, conveyor,
SKU, changeover—not vague efficiency or innovation language.

Andrew's verified University of Toronto and Automata robotics context may be
used briefly during discovery to explain why he is asking. It must not disguise
commercial intent once the conversation reaches requirements, economics,
prototype work, a pilot, or an LOI. The first email does not pitch a finished
robot or claim installations and savings that do not exist.

Concrete nouns must remain evidence-bound. When the account facts establish
food production but not trays, cases, pouches, conveyors, or pallet patterns,
the first note asks about a physical packing or handling task and lets the
operator name it. Specificity is not permission to invent the station.
